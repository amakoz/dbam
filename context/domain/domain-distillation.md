## Summary

Dbam is a screening-reminder product: from a short health profile it computes which preventive screenings from a curated catalog apply to an adult in Poland, labels each by importance tier, and follows each one through the loop "recommendation → appointment date → confirmed completion → next due date" (`context/foundation/prd.md:150`) with opt-in emails. The **Core** is two subdomains: _screening recommendation_ (eligibility, tier, interval over the catalog) and _screening follow-through_ (the user's plan, appointment, done/confirm record, due-again and the emails that prompt each step). Of 50 Core and Supporting terms, **4 are missing in code** — most importantly the user's own _exam_ (`badanie` for one user), which has no type: its identity is just `(user_id, catalog_slug)` spread across two tables, three reminder ledgers and a read-time partition rule. The strongest drift is around that implicit exam: appointment reminders fire for plans whose catalog entry is retired or demoted to draft (D-01), a catalog snapshot can demote an active entry and silently hide users' records (D-02), and "mark done" is two separate writes (D-03). **#1 is the "exam for a user" aggregate** because its invariants are split across the route handler, two SQL functions, three ledgers and a TS read rule, and three of the drift rows land on it. The Polish word `badanie` names both the catalog screening and the user's exam, and `due` carries five meanings in code; the glossary separates them.

Drift by fix side: **code 3, docs 0, decision 5**. Open questions: **10**.
